Physics-based Hierarchical Adaptive Spatial Evasion

From a billiard-ball idea to three flying quadcopters

PHASE treats every vehicle as a physical object: the heavier one keeps its line, the lighter one gets out of the way. This page follows the algorithm from its first upload, through three kinematic versions, to closed-loop flights on PX4 and the dynamics-aware PHASE-D.

Concept3 Sep 2026PHASE_V1.0.py

Vehicles as billiard balls

The first stable upload set the idea: plan each vehicle's route with localized A*, and only escalate when two vehicles' safety bubbles overlap. Then the lower-momentum vehicle sidesteps 90°, and a geometric solver (PBD) pushes any remaining overlap apart. Momentum is priority × speed, like the mass of a ball.

Intentlocal A*→ Conflictbubbles overlap→ Evasion90° sidestep→ PBDpush apart→ Commit

Model

  • Kinematic point masses stepped at dt = 0.2 s; positions are set directly, so there is no acceleration limit.
  • momentum = priority × speed. In a conflict, the share of the evasion each vehicle takes is weighted by the other's momentum.
  • Safety bubble: marginᵢ + marginⱼ + 0.2 m, checked on the positions proposed for the next tick.
  • Deflection: the 90° left or right turn whose target point is better for clearance and obstacles.

V1.013 Sep 2026safedeadlocks

A test suite puts it under pressure

An autonomous test harness ran V1.0 on 53 cases: 23 hand-built adversarial scenarios plus 30 seeded random ones (mixed traffic, crossings, convergence). Vehicles never got closer than allowed. The failures were vehicles that never arrived: symmetric encounters left them stuck facing each other.

Mechanics

  • Conflict fires when bubbles overlap and at least one of the pair is NORMAL; both get a DEFLECTING target and follow it until reached, then replan with A*.
  • Global micro-PBD (“concrete-bounce fixed”): 50 iterations every tick, obstacles first, then vehicle pairs, momentum-weighted.

V1.114 Sep 2026more arrivalssafety regression

Steer once, replan globally, and a lesson

V1.1 attacked the stand-offs. Each conflict now steers only once per event, and after it every vehicle replans with A* from its corrected position. More vehicles arrived (20 of 22 cases, up from 18), but the geometric solver now only ran when a new conflict appeared. Between those moments nothing kept vehicles apart, and they touched.

V1.214 Sep 2026safe againfast

Safety every tick, replanning only when needed

V1.2 keeps V1.1's event-based steering but runs the solver on every tick again, and replans only when a new conflict appears. It also detects deadlocks: equal-momentum pairs get a tie-break, and vehicles stuck in place are reported. The result has V1.0's safety at V1.1's speed, in 768 lines.

Intentlocal A*→ Conflict event+ 1.5× tie-break→ PBD1000 iterations, every tick→ A* replanonly on new conflict→ Commit+ deadlock detection
18 / 22shared deterministic cases passed
0separation-violation ticks
8.2 mclosest approach
3.7 smean delay, half of V1.0's

What changed

  • Phase 3 runs unconditionally: max_pbd_iters = 1000, obstacles first, then pairs.
  • Symmetric deadlock: when two momenta are equal, the first vehicle's momentum is boosted 1.5× for the steering share and the PBD weights (logged once per pair).
  • Positional deadlock: a vehicle that moves less than 0.1 m for over 3 s is reported, and its recovery is logged.
  • Phase 4 replans every active vehicle, but only on ticks with a new conflict.

Still failing

03_equal_head_on, 04_three_way_cross, 06_diagonal_cross and 16_grazing: all safe, but not every vehicle arrives. Deadlocks are now identified, not yet resolved.

PX4 test9–10 Nov 2026V1.2 unchangedviolations

Real flight dynamics enter the loop

Three PX4 quadcopters (PX4 v1.17 SIH, software-in-the-loop) flew three scenarios at 10 m, while V1.2 ran live on their measured positions and sent offboard setpoints. In the kinematic sim, PBD moves a vehicle instantly. A real quadcopter needs time and distance to change velocity, so the corrections arrived too late.

Harness (px4_sitl/ and phase_d/)

  • phase_stepper.py loads V1.2 unmodified and runs its loop one tick at a time. test_stepper_equivalence.py proves it reproduces Simulation.run() exactly.
  • launch_sih.py starts one SIH instance per drone (airframe 10040, -i N, spawn via PX4_HOME_LAT/LON).
  • phase_px4_bridge.py: MAVSDK 3.17.4, take-off, shared ENU frame, offboard set_position_velocity_ned at 5 Hz, paced on PX4's simulation clock (SIH ran at about 0.83× real time).
  • Scenarios: three_way_swap, head_on_cross (priorities 10 / 1 / 5), obstacle_choke (6 m cylinder). Speed 4 m/s, margin 2.5 m.

Findings

  • F-01: V1.2 violates separation on real dynamics. PBD's instant position corrections cannot be flown.
  • F-02: kinematic livelock in the symmetric three-way swap.
  • F-03: a deflection lasts one tick, because the replan on the same tick resets it.

Full log: CHANGELOG.md (entries H-01…H-11, F-01…F-03).

PHASE-D10 Nov 2026experimental0 violations on PX4

Billiard balls that know how to brake

PHASE-D keeps PHASE's ideas and adds the physics a real vehicle has. Each safety ball grows with closing speed, reaction time and braking distance. The lighter vehicle brakes first, and the heavier one brakes only for what remains. Sidesteps run to completion, and every new one follows the same rule. Neighbours come from a spatial grid, so the cost grows with nearby vehicles rather than with every pair.

Spatial gridneighbours only→ Growing ballsspeed + lag + braking→ Deflectall neighbours, to completion→ Priority brakinglighter yields first→ Accel limitsbraking first→ PBDlast resort, bounded→ Commit

Equations

Growing ball      Rᵢ = marginᵢ + cᵢ·(t_latency + τ) + cᵢ² / (2·a_brake)
                  cᵢ = max(0, vᵢ·û)   (closing speed towards the other object)

Safe closing      c_safe(g) = a_brake · ( −t + √(t² + 2g / a_brake) ),  t = t_latency + τ

Lag-aware gap     g_eff = g − max(0, c_meas − c_cmd) · τ

Priority braking  excess = c_cmd − c_safe(g_eff)
                  lower momentum removes it first, higher removes the rest;
                  obstacles never yield

What the evidence asks for next

PHASE-D is safe in these flights but still busy: about half of its re-deflections end because a stall timer runs out, not because the conflict is solved. The items below come from the known limitations; none of them has been built or tested yet.

Churnsidestep relative to the goal, stall timer that allows for braking, keep the partner as an A* wall after a conflict
Marginexplicit tracking-error allowance at obstacles (now 2.84–2.88 m vs 2.7 m)
Evidencemore flights per scenario; find the cause of PX4's 3.1–3.6 m/s cruise vs 4 m/s commanded
Scale3D (not altitude-locked), larger PX4 fleets, then Gazebo and hardware

Why churn happens (from run analysis)

  • Large balls keep pairs “in conflict” for most of an encounter.
  • Braking slows a deflecting vehicle, the 1 s stall timer ends the deflection, and the still-active conflict starts a new one.
  • Each 90° turn is relative to the current heading, so turns compound into zigzags.
  • After a conflict clears, A* heads straight back through the other vehicle's area.

Physics check

Real billiards explain the lighter vehicle yielding more and braking along the line of centres. They do not explain 90° turns relative to heading, repeated collisions with the same partner (the goal-seeking A* acts like a bowl with no friction) or the stall-timer loop.

Scaling

At 1,000 vehicles a tick takes 208 ms in Python, right at the 200 ms real-time budget, so larger fleets need a faster implementation.

Reading the evidence

What was actually run

Kinematic suite

V1.0–V1.2 suite results come from PHASE's own simulator: vehicles move exactly where the algorithm puts them. It tests the logic, not flight.

PX4 SIH

The PX4 flights are software-in-the-loop: the real PX4 flight stack with a built-in physics model, on one Mac. They are not Gazebo, and not hardware.

Sample size

Two PX4 flights per scenario, three vehicles, altitude-locked 2D. Enough to show the V1.2 failure clearly, too few for statistics.
